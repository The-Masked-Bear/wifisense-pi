================================================================================
  WiFi SENSE  --  seeing motion, presence and breathing through walls with WiFi
================================================================================

A Raspberry Pi 4 and an ESP32-S3 that together detect people in a room using
nothing but ordinary WiFi signals. No camera, no PIR sensor, nothing worn. It
works through interior walls, in complete darkness, and it detects a person who
is sitting perfectly still by picking up the movement of their chest as they
breathe.

Everything below is written to be read at the bench, away from the terminal.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  WHAT THIS ACTUALLY DOES  (and what it does not)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WORKS, MEASURED ON THIS HARDWARE:

  * Motion detection        walking, gesturing, sitting down.  Reads ~28 dB
                            above the noise floor -- unmissable.
  * Presence detection      distinguishes an empty room from a room with a
                            motionless person in it.  This is the hard one, and
                            the thing PIR sensors get wrong constantly.
  * Breathing rate          8 to 36 breaths per minute.  Validated to within
                            0.1 bpm against known ground truth.
  * Through walls           2.4 GHz passes through plasterboard, wood and glass
                            with little loss.  Brick and concrete attenuate it
                            heavily; foil-backed insulation blocks it.
  * Activity classification empty / still / subtle / active / vigorous.
  * Environment sensing     temperature, humidity, barometric pressure and a
                            gas-based air quality index, from a BMP280, a
                            DHT22 and an MQ135 on the same node.  Read section
                            6d before trusting the air quality number: it
                            cannot see PM2.5, which is what a real AQI is
                            mostly made of.

DOES NOT WORK, AND CANNOT WITH THIS HARDWARE:

  * Imaging or pose.  You will not get a skeleton or a silhouette. The MIT
    demos that show that (RF-Pose, RF-Capture, WiTrack) do not use WiFi -- they
    use purpose-built FMCW radar with GHz of bandwidth and a physical antenna
    array. WiFi at 20 MHz bandwidth gives a range resolution of c/2B = 7.5
    metres, which is larger than most rooms. There is no signal processing that
    recovers an image from that; the information is not present.

  * Locating a person in the room.  One transmitter and one receiver measure a
    single path. You learn that something changed, not where. Coarse
    localisation needs three or more spatially separated nodes.

  * Counting people.  Two people produce a bigger disturbance than one, but not
    a reliably countable one.

  * Detecting someone holding perfectly still AND holding their breath. There
    is genuinely nothing to measure. This is physics, not a limitation of the
    code.

  * Identifying who someone is.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.  HOW IT WORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A WiFi signal reaches the receiver by many paths at once -- direct, plus
reflections off walls, furniture and people. Those copies add up with different
delays, so some frequencies reinforce and others cancel. The resulting
"fingerprint" across frequency is called Channel State Information (CSI), and
most WiFi chips can report it.

Move anything in the room and the path lengths change, so the fingerprint
changes. A body is mostly water and reflects 2.4 GHz strongly, which is what
makes people visible. At 2.4 GHz one wavelength is 12.5 cm, so a chest moving
6 mm while breathing shifts the path by a measurable fraction of a wavelength.

The data path:

    ESP32-S3  --- captures CSI, 64 sub-carriers per packet, 100 times a second
       |          (needs a packet to measure; see section 3)
       | USB serial, 921600 baud, COBS-framed with CRC-16
       v
    Pi 4      --- cleans it up, then answers two questions:
       |
       |          "is something moving?"   energy at 1.5-9 Hz
       |          "is someone there?"      energy at 0.13-0.7 Hz (breathing)
       |
       | WebSocket, ~330 kbit/s
       v
    Browser   --- live dashboard, on the Pi or on your phone

WHY THOSE TWO FREQUENCY BANDS

Both are Doppler frequencies, not body-movement frequencies. A reflector moving
at velocity v shifts the signal by v/wavelength. Walking at 1 m/s therefore
writes energy at about 8 Hz, not at walking pace. Breathing moves the chest
slowly enough that it stays down at 0.13-0.7 Hz, which is why the two are
separable at all.

WHAT IT MEASURES AGAINST

Every reading is in dB above the receiver's own noise floor, so an empty room
reads 0 dB in any room, at any distance, at any transmit power. There is no
calibration step and no learned baseline that can drift.

Finding a trustworthy noise floor is the whole trick. Receiver noise is
independent on every sub-carrier because it is generated inside the radio;
anything happening in the room changes all the sub-carriers together. So the
sub-carrier covariance is decomposed, and the weakest directions -- which the
room cannot reach -- define the noise level.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.  THE PART THAT SURPRISES PEOPLE:  YOU MUST GENERATE TRAFFIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CSI only exists when a packet is *received*. A silent channel produces no data
at all, no matter how good the hardware is. Three approaches were measured
here:

    approach                                        measured CSI rate
    ---------------------------------------------   -----------------
    Listen passively to ambient WiFi                 ~1 Hz, irregular
    ESP32 transmits, capture CSI from the ACKs       ~1 Hz  (does not work)
    >> Pi sends UDP to the ESP32 <<                  1:1, up to 200 Hz

The third is what this system does, and it is why the ESP32 must be joined to
your WiFi network. The Pi sends a small UDP packet to the node 100 times a
second; each arrives as an ordinary data frame and yields exactly one CSI
sample. The sample rate is therefore chosen, not discovered.

Passive sniffing at 1 Hz is far too slow for breathing detection, which needs
20 Hz or more. That mode still exists as a fallback but it is not useful for
much beyond coarse motion.

4.  WIRING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1  TRANSPORT: THE RADIO LINK IS LIVE
     ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     CSI travels from the ESP32-S3 to the Pi over the **nRF24L01+ radio**.  The
     USB cable carries only power, plus a duplicate of the status/log frames so
     the node stays diagnosable if the radio is misconfigured or out of range.

     MEASURED, over the air:

         sample rate        ~99 Hz sustained  (100 Hz requested)
         CRC errors         0
         frame integrity    every frame that arrives is intact
         range              whole-room at +20 dBm PA

     Switching transport, either end, at runtime:

         cd pi
         ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py link nrf24    # radio (default)
         ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py link cc1101   # sub-GHz, out of band
         ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py link usb      # back to the cable

     The choice is stored in the node's flash and restored at boot.  The Pi's
     side follows "link" in pi/config.json.

     FIVE BUGS HAD TO BE FIXED TO GET HERE, all worth knowing if it misbehaves:

       1. SPI clock.  The RF24 library defaults to 10 MHz, which dupont jumpers
          will not carry; a bit-banged probe at 250 kHz read the chip perfectly
          while the library reported "no chip".  Now pinned to 1 MHz.
       2. Cold-boot detection.  The chip's SPI state machine can come up
          mid-word and misread every command.  radio_begin() now clocks a full
          bit-banged transaction first to resynchronise it -- without this the
          node silently fell back to USB on every reboot.
       3. Transmit inside a mutex.  radio_send() was called while holding the
          lock that guards frame encoding, so a slow radio stalled the status
          task and the command handler and the whole node went mute.
       4. Receive FIFO.  The Pi read one packet per poll.  A frame is five
          fragments, so 100 Hz is 500 packets/s against a 3-deep FIFO -- 19%
          packet loss compounded to 66% frame loss.  It now drains the FIFO.
       5. Channel.  The original 108 (2508 MHz) sits outside the ISM band, where
          the module's antenna match and filter fall off; delivery was 25-33%
          regardless of rate.  Now 80 (2480 MHz), inside ISM and clear of the
          WiFi channel in use.

     CC1101 STATUS: the chip responds on SPI (VERSION 0x14) and the transmit
     path is implemented, but the radio link has only been proven end to end on
     the nRF24.  Its GDO pins read as unpowered, so check its 3.3 V before
     relying on it.

4.1a RADIO LINK TUNING  (how delivery got from 72% to ~88%)
     ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     A CSI frame is split across several radio packets, and the frame survives
     only if EVERY packet does -- delivery = (1-p)^n.  That makes both the
     packet loss p and the fragment count n worth attacking.

       auto-ack ON            A lost packet was previously lost forever.  A
                              32-byte packet is ~128 us at 2 Mbit/s, so even
                              600 packets/s is ~8% duty: there is ample room to
                              retry.  setRetries(1,3) bounds a packet at ~2 ms
                              so a deaf receiver cannot stall the sender.
                              72% -> 85%.

       trim sub-carriers      The radio reports 64 LLTF sub-carriers but only
                              52 carry signal; the Pi discarded the rest on
                              arrival anyway.  Dropping them at the node cuts
                              the payload 128 -> 104 bytes and the frame from 6
                              fragments to 5 -- ~17% fewer packets.
                              85% -> ~88%.

       duplicate fragments    Auto-ack retransmits when the ACKNOWLEDGEMENT is
                              lost rather than the data, so the receiver
                              legitimately sees a fragment twice.  The
                              reassembler treated that as corruption and threw
                              the whole frame away, which reported far more
                              "lost" frames than were ever sent.  Duplicates
                              are now ignored.

     Remaining loss is largely the nRF24's 3-deep RX FIFO: packets are
     acknowledged in hardware on arrival, so anything overwritten before Python
     reads it is lost invisibly and auto-ack cannot recover it.  Sending fewer,
     smaller packets is the only real lever, which is what the trim does.

     ~88 Hz delivered is far above what the detectors need (respiration wants
     ~20 Hz), so this is comfortable rather than marginal.


4.1b RADIO LINK ENCRYPTION
     ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     Everything sent over the radio is encrypted with AES-128 in counter mode.

     This is not decoration.  CSI reveals when a room is occupied, when people
     move, and when they sleep.  In the clear, anyone within range with a
     matching radio on the same channel reads all of it.

     Encrypted radio frame, before fragmentation:

         [ session u32 ][ counter u32 ][ ciphertext ... ]

     The 8-byte header is in the clear -- the receiver needs it to rebuild the
     counter block -- and leaks only a random session id and a frame counter.
     The CTR nonce is session || counter || eight zero bytes.

     Counter-mode security depends entirely on never reusing a (key, nonce)
     pair, so: the counter is 32 bits (497 days at 100 Hz) and the node draws a
     fresh random session id at every boot, so a reset opens a new nonce space
     rather than replaying the last one.  The receiver also rejects any counter
     it has already accepted, which stops a captured frame being replayed back
     at it later.

     THIS IS CONFIDENTIALITY, NOT AUTHENTICATION.  A passive listener learns
     nothing.  The CRC16 inside the encrypted payload means a random forgery
     survives with probability 1/65536 -- proportionate for a home sensor, but
     it is not a MAC, so do not rely on it where an active attacker matters.

     CHANGING THE KEY (do this; the shipped one is in the repository):

         python3 -c "import secrets;print(secrets.token_hex(16))"

     Put the same value in BOTH:
         firmware/src/config.h   RADIO_KEY  (as {0x.., 0x.., ...})
         pi/config.json          radio_key  (as 32 hex characters)
     then reflash the node.  A key mismatch shows up as cobs_errors climbing
     with frames_ok stuck at zero.

     Set RADIO_ENCRYPT to 0 and radio_encrypt to false to send in the clear;
     useful only while debugging a link.

     ONE TRAP WORTH KNOWING: the nRF24 uses fixed-size payloads, so the last
     fragment of every frame is padded.  Unencrypted that padding is zeros and
     the framer skips it harmlessly; encrypted, the same padding decrypts to
     random bytes that corrupt the *next* frame.  The receiver therefore trims
     each reassembled frame at its COBS terminator.  Symptom if that breaks:
     the link works in the clear and fails the instant encryption is enabled.


4.1d CC1101 STATUS  (works in software; blocked on RF)
     ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     The CC1101 path is complete and correct in software.  It is driven by
     firmware/src/cc1101_driver.{h,cpp} -- an in-tree register driver, not a
     library.  The ELECHOUSE/SmartRC library could not be used: it drives the
     global Arduino SPI object, latches an "already initialised" flag, and
     busy-waits on GDO0 for up to 500 ms per packet.  On this board both radios
     share SPI2, so that library made switching between them either fail or
     hang the node outright.

     VERIFIED WORKING:
       * nrf24 <-> cc1101 switching, 5 consecutive swaps, no hangs
       * Pi receiver opens 10/10 (was ~30% before the slow-open fix)
       * receiver holds RX continuously (rearms 0 over a 40 s run)
       * all 35 modem registers identical on both ends, byte for byte
       * PATABLE 0xC0 (max power for 433 MHz), FREND0 selects it
       * sync words detected off the air, so RF genuinely arrives

     BLOCKED ON:
       Signal strength.  With the node transmitting 912 packets during the
       sample:

           433 MHz quiet          -102.5 dBm mean, -98.0 peak
           node transmitting      -102.3 dBm mean, -94.0 peak
           lift                    +4.0 dB

       At 30 cm with +10 dBm output, free-space loss is only ~15 dB, so the
       receiver should see about -5 dBm.  It sees -94 dBm: a shortfall of
       ~89 dB.  Packets occasionally sync but always fail CRC, which is exactly
       what a signal sitting at the noise floor does.

       A missing or unmatched antenna costs 20-40 dB per end; ~89 dB is
       consistent with both ends radiating from a bare pad.  Worth checking:
         * the antenna is soldered/screwed to the ANT pad, not just near it
         * it is a 433 MHz antenna (868/915 MHz on a 433 module is a severe
           mismatch and looks exactly like this)
         * the module's ground plane connection

       Once RF is healthy, no code changes are needed:
           cd pi && ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py link cc1101

       Throughput ceiling when it works: ~22 Hz at 38.4 kBaud, which is enough
       for motion and breathing but not the 100 Hz the nRF24 carries.


4.1c IF A RADIO LINK GOES SILENT
     ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     A radio that initialises but delivers nothing would leave the Pi blind,
     and in radio mode the serial port is not even open to ask the node why.
     So if the configured radio link delivers nothing at all for 60 seconds,
     the Pi switches itself to the USB cable and commands the node back to
     "link usb".  It only fires when the link has produced *nothing* since
     starting -- a working link that merely stalls is left alone.

     The dashboard shows "fell_back_from" when this has happened.


4.2  ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 N16R8  --  FORBIDDEN PINS
     ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     Read this before wiring anything to the ESP32.

       GPIO 26-32   internal SPI flash.        NEVER USE.
       GPIO 33-37   octal PSRAM.               NEVER USE on the N16R8.
       GPIO 0       boot strapping pin.        avoid
       GPIO 3,45,46 strapping pins.            avoid
       GPIO 19,20   native USB D-/D+.          avoid
       GPIO 43,44   UART0 console (in use).    avoid

     The "R8" in N16R8 means 8 MB of *octal* PSRAM, and octal PSRAM consumes
     GPIO 33-37. Wiring a peripheral there produces a board that fails to boot
     with no useful error. This is the most common way to brick this module.

     Safe to use: 1, 2, 4-18, 21, 38-42, 47, 48
     (38 or 48 may drive the on-board RGB LED depending on board revision.)

4.4  POWER  --  where these builds usually fail
     ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     The nRF24L01+ PA/LNA is the problem child. It draws ~115 mA spikes at
     +20 dBm and the Pi's 3.3 V rail cannot supply that transient. The symptom
     is distinctive: it works for a few packets, then goes silent for good.

       * Solder 10-100 uF electrolytic PLUS 100 nF ceramic directly across the
         module's own VCC/GND pins. At the module -- not on a breadboard rail.
       * Better: feed it from its own AMS1117-3.3 regulator off 5 V.
       * The same applies to the ESP32 dev board's regulator.

     CC1101 is gentle (~30 mA); 100 nF + 10 uF is plenty.

     Voltage tolerance: nRF24 VCC must be 3.3 V but its logic pins tolerate
     5 V. The CC1101 tolerates 5 V nowhere. 3.3 V only, on every pin.

     Antennas: attach before powering up. Transmitting into an open port can
     damage the power amplifier. Match the CC1101 antenna to the module's band
     -- 433 and 868 MHz antennas are not interchangeable.

     IMPORTANT -- BOTH ENDS NEED A RADIO. A CC1101 link needs a CC1101 at the
     Pi *and* at the ESP32. Same for the nRF24. One module is half a link.


4.5  WHICH RADIO IS FOR WHAT
     ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     ESP32-S3 WiFi    the sensor itself. This is what measures the room.

     USB serial       always available, and the diagnostic path: status and log
                      frames stay on the cable whatever the CSI link is doing.
                      119 kbit/s of CSI on a link with ~85% headroom, zero
                      errors measured, and it adds nothing to the airwaves.

     CC1101 (433/868) the best wireless option, because it is completely
                      OUTSIDE the 2.4 GHz band being sensed and so cannot
                      disturb the measurement. Carries full raw CSI at up to
                      ~169 Hz at 500 kbit/s, ~92 Hz at 250 kbit/s.

     nRF24L01+        what this build actually runs (see 4.1).  Fast enough for
                      raw CSI, but it transmits in 2.4 GHz -- the exact band
                      being measured -- so the channel has to stay clear of the
                      AP in use.  It is pinned to channel 80 (2480 MHz): inside
                      the ISM band, where the module's antenna match and filter
                      are actually specified, and clear of the WiFi channel in
                      use.  Measured delivery ~88-91% of 100 Hz, which is four
                      times what respiration needs.

     For a node sitting next to the Pi the USB cable is simpler and lossless.
     For a node placed where the sensing geometry wants it -- across the room
     from the router -- the radio is what makes that placement possible, and is
     why this build uses it.


4.6  ENVIRONMENT SENSORS  --  BMP280, DHT22, MQ135
     ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
     All three hang off the ESP32-S3, not the Pi. That is forced, not a
     preference:

       * The MQ135 is an ANALOGUE part and the Pi 4 has no ADC at all. Reading
         it from the Pi would need an external ADC chip.
       * The Pi's SPI0 exposes exactly two hardware chip selects, CE0 and CE1,
         and both are already taken (nRF24 on CE0, CC1101 on CE1). There is no
         third slot for the BMP280 without bit-banging a soft chip select.

     BMP280  (pressure + temperature, SPI)

       Shares SPI2 with the two radios and only needs a chip select of its own.

         module pin        ESP32-S3     note
         ---------------   ---------    -------------------------------------
         VCC               3V3          3.3 V. Not 5 V.
         GND               GND
         SCL / SCK         GPIO12       shared with both radios
         SDA / SDI / MOSI  GPIO11       shared with both radios
         SDO / MISO        GPIO13       shared with both radios
         CSB / CS          GPIO15       this module's own chip select

       Labelling varies by breakout. In SPI mode SDA is the *input* (MOSI) and
       SDO is the *output* (MISO), which is the opposite of what the I2C-style
       names suggest. If the chip id read fails, swap those two first.

       A BME280 works in this slot too -- same registers, chip id 0x60 instead
       of 0x58 -- but only its temperature and pressure are read, because the
       DHT22 already covers humidity.

     DHT22 / AM2302  (temperature + humidity, 1-wire)

         module pin   ESP32-S3
         ----------   ---------
         VCC          3V3
         OUT / DATA   GPIO16
         GND          GND

       The 3-pin breakout boards already carry the required pull-up resistor.
       A bare 4-pin sensor does not: add 10 kOhm from DATA to 3V3, or it never
       answers.

       This part is slow and fussy by design. It refuses to be read faster than
       once every 2 s and self-heats if pushed, which is why the firmware polls
       it every 3 s and retries up to three times before calling it failed.

     MQ135  ("Flying Fish" gas module, analogue)

         module pin   ESP32-S3     note
         ----------   ---------    ---------------------------------------
         VCC          5V           the heater is specified for 5 V
         GND          GND
         AO           GPIO4        VIA A DIVIDER -- see below
         DO           n/c          on-board comparator, not used

       AO CANNOT GO STRAIGHT TO THE ESP32. Powered from 5 V, that pin can
       output up to 5 V, and the ESP32-S3's ADC tolerates 3.3 V. Two equal
       resistors halve it:

              MQ135 AO ----[ 10k ]----+----[ 10k ]---- GND
                                      |
                                      +----> ESP32 GPIO4   (2.5 V max)

       Any matched pair works -- 10k/10k, 4.7k/4.7k, 100k/100k all give exactly
       half. Only the ratio matters. Unequal resistors the wrong way round can
       exceed 3.3 V and destroy the input.

       ALTERNATIVE, NO RESISTORS: power the module from 3V3 instead and wire AO
       direct. Electrically safe, because AO can never exceed its own supply.
       Then tell the node about it, once, and it remembers:

           python tools/node.py mq135 supply 3v3

       The trade-off is real: the heater is designed for 5 V and runs cool at
       3.3 V, so the reading still moves in the right direction but the
       absolute figure drifts more.

       GPIO4 IS NOT ARBITRARY. It must be one of GPIO1-10, which are ADC1.
       ADC2 (GPIO11-20) is physically unusable whenever WiFi is running -- the
       radio owns that converter and a read returns an error instead of a
       value. Since WiFi is the entire point of this node, ADC2 is permanently
       off the table.

       POWER: the heater draws ~150 mA continuously and stays warm to the
       touch. That is a large, constant load. If the ESP32 browns out or the
       CSI rate becomes erratic after adding this sensor, feed it from a
       separate 5 V supply with a common ground rather than through the dev
       board.

       WARM-UP AND BURN-UP. Two different things, both real:
         * Every power-on needs ~3 minutes before the reading stops sliding.
           The firmware waits this out before it will auto-calibrate.
         * A brand-new sensor needs 24-48 HOURS of continuous power before its
           baseline settles for good. Until then, recalibrate occasionally.

     PIN SUMMARY  --  everything now attached to the ESP32-S3

       GPIO4    MQ135 AO (through the divider)
       GPIO9    nRF24 IRQ
       GPIO10   nRF24 CSN
       GPIO11   SPI MOSI   (nRF24 + CC1101 + BMP280)
       GPIO12   SPI SCK    (nRF24 + CC1101 + BMP280)
       GPIO13   SPI MISO   (nRF24 + CC1101 + BMP280)
       GPIO14   nRF24 CE
       GPIO15   BMP280 CS
       GPIO16   DHT22 DATA
       GPIO17   CC1101 GDO2
       GPIO18   CC1101 GDO0
       GPIO21   CC1101 CSN

     WHY THIS DOES NOT DISTURB THE CSI MEASUREMENT

       The sensors are sampled by one low-priority task pinned to CPU core 1.
       The WiFi driver and the CSI callback live on core 0. That split matters
       for exactly one reason: reading a DHT22 requires masking interrupts for
       about 5 ms, because the part encodes its bits as pulse *widths* of 26 us
       versus 70 us and a task switch mid-bit rewrites the data. Masking
       interrupts on the ESP32 affects only the calling core, so on core 1 that
       5 ms costs a few queued CSI frames; on core 0 it would punch a hole in
       the CSI stream every three seconds.

       Measured after adding all three: 113.6 Hz captured at the node, 108.1 Hz
       delivered over the encrypted nRF24 link, 95.1% end to end, and ZERO
       node-side queue drops.

     WHY THE ENVIRONMENT FRAME IS SENT THREE TIMES

       Each reading is transmitted once per second for three seconds, so the
       Pi gets about three chances at every sample. It discards the repeats by
       the node's own sample timestamp, so they never reach the history or the
       display.

       This is not belt-and-braces. Measured: with the CSI stream idle every
       environment frame arrived, one every 3.2 s, with zero reassembly drops.
       With CSI running at 100 Hz the radio carries ~500 packets a second, and
       under that contention roughly TWO IN THREE environment frames were lost
       -- the panel showed air that was 10 to 12 seconds stale.

       That loss rate is nearly invisible in the CSI stream, where a hundred
       frames arrive every second and a missing one changes nothing. It is very
       visible in a frame that only arrives every three seconds.

       Retransmitting costs six extra packets per three seconds against about
       fifteen hundred -- 0.4% of the link. Spacing the copies a second apart
       matters, because the loss is bursty: three copies sent back-to-back
       would be lost together.

       After the change: mean gap 3.0 s, worst 5.3 s, and CSI throughput
       unchanged at 94 Hz with zero rejected frames.

5.  SETUP FROM SCRATCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Already done on this Pi; recorded so it can be rebuilt.

5.1  Python environment  (Debian 13 blocks pip into system Python, PEP 668,
     so a venv is required; --system-site-packages reuses the apt numpy)

       cd "/home/pi/Desktop/Claude/smart thing"
       python3 -m venv --system-site-packages .venv
       ./.venv/bin/pip install scipy fastapi "uvicorn[standard]" websockets \
                               pyserial cc1101

5.2  Flash the firmware

       cd firmware
       ~/.local/bin/pio run -t upload --upload-port /dev/ttyUSB0

5.3  Tell the node which WiFi to join  (2.4 GHz only -- the ESP32-S3 cannot
     see 5 GHz networks)

       cd pi
       ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py wifi "YourSSID" "YourPassword"

     Credentials are stored in the ESP32's flash and survive power cycles, so
     this is a one-time step. No recompile is needed to change networks.

5.4  Autostart at boot

       ./install.sh              server only
       ./install.sh --kiosk      server plus a full-screen browser on the Pi
       ./install.sh --uninstall  remove

6.  RUNNING IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The service is installed and starts automatically at boot.

    http://localhost:8080          on the Pi
    http://<pi-address>:8080       from a phone or laptop on the same network

    systemctl status wifisense     is it running
    journalctl -u wifisense -f     live logs
    sudo systemctl restart wifisense

Run it by hand instead (stop the service first):

    cd pi
    ../.venv/bin/python -m wifisense --link serial
    ../.venv/bin/python -m wifisense --link synthetic   # no hardware needed

Switching transport (needs radios wired to the ESP32 as well as the Pi):

    cd pi
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py link nrf24     # 2.4 GHz, ch 108 / 2508 MHz
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py link cc1101    # 433 MHz, out of band
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py link usb       # back to the cable
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py radiosweep     # hunt for radios on any pin
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py radioscan      # probe the documented pins

The choice is stored in the node's flash and survives power cycles. Status and
log frames always stay on the USB cable regardless, so a node whose radio is
misconfigured or out of range can still be diagnosed.

Talking to the node directly:

    cd pi
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py info
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py monitor 30
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py wifi "SSID" "password"
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py mode sniffer
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py reboot

Environment sensors:

    cd pi
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py env                # all three, right now
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py mq135 cal          # baseline the air quality
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py mq135 supply 3v3   # if AO is wired direct
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py mq135 supply 5v    # if AO goes via a divider

Both mq135 settings are stored in the node's flash. Changing the supply clears
the stored baseline, because the resistance scale it was calibrated against has
just changed and keeping it would leave every reading wrong by a constant
factor with nothing on screen to say so.

Note: opening the serial port resets the board, so it is unreachable for the
first few seconds. tools/node.py waits for it; a raw terminal will not.

Checking the signal processing against known ground truth:

    cd pi
    ../.venv/bin/python tools/validate_dsp.py

This runs the full detector chain against synthetic CSI whose breathing rate
is known exactly, and is the only way to confirm the numbers are right -- a
real room cannot tell you whether "14 breaths/min" was correct.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6b. HOW RESPIRATION IS TRACKED  (and three bugs that made it jump around)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The rate is TRACKED across windows, not re-decided from scratch each time.
Three faults in the original made the reading unusable, all fixed:

  1. NO CONTINUITY.  A plain argmax over the spectrum picks a fresh winner
     every window, so whenever two peaks are comparable -- normal for a weak or
     slightly contaminated signal -- the estimate flips between them.  Measured
     live it went 8.8 -> 25.5 -> 10.5 bpm in under a minute, which no lung does.
     Now a peak near the tracked rate keeps the lock unless a rival is clearly
     stronger (RIVAL_FRAC), and the rate follows by a slow EMA.

  2. NO HYSTERESIS.  Validity was a single threshold on confidence, so as
     confidence hovered around it the flag chattered on and off every few
     seconds.  The motion detector had hysteresis from the start for exactly
     this reason; respiration never got any.  Now VALID_ON/VALID_OFF, plus
     several windows must agree (AGREE_NEEDED) before anything is published.

  3. TWITCHY CONFIDENCE.  The per-window peak-prominence measure is
     legitimately noisy -- on a subject sitting perfectly still with a rock
     steady rate it was seen collapsing 0.36 -> 0.06 and back within seconds.
     It is now smoothed asymmetrically: quick to rise so a genuine lock
     publishes promptly, slow to fall so a single bad window cannot tear it
     down.

  Also: gross motion above RELOCK_MOTION_MAX_DB will confirm an existing rate
  but never adopt a NEW one, because at that level a limb swing produces a
  perfectly convincing periodic peak that has nothing to do with breathing.
  And a brief movement no longer discards the lock -- the subject's rate has
  not changed just because they shifted in their chair.

  Measured after the fixes, over 90 s: 0 jumps above 3 bpm (was 7), 2 validity
  flips (was 12).  Held 8.9-10.5 bpm steadily once the subject was still.

  WHAT TO EXPECT: it needs you still and about 30 s.  While you are moving it
  says "subject moving" and publishes nothing, which is correct -- movement
  changes the channel by orders of magnitude more than a chest does.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6c. IF BREATHING WILL NOT SHOW  (read this before assuming it is broken)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Almost every "it won't detect my breathing" case is geometry, not software.

WHERE TO SIT.  The system measures the path BETWEEN the router and the ESP32.
Sitting NEXT TO the node is the wrong place: you are outside the volume being
measured, while your small movements are hugely amplified by proximity.  Sit
BETWEEN the two, ideally near the midpoint.

HOW FAR APART.  Check the RSSI on the dashboard:

    -40 to -60 dBm    the useful working range
    stronger than -35 dBm   the node is too close to the router
    weaker than -85 dBm     too little signal

Too strong is a real failure mode, and a counter-intuitive one.  The system
senses a body by how it perturbs the sum of all paths.  When the direct
router-to-node path overwhelms everything, your reflection is a tiny fraction
of the total and gets buried.  Moving the node further from the router
deliberately weakens the direct path so the reflections -- which is where you
live -- carry proportionally more of the signal.

HOW STILL.  Watch the MOTION reading, not your own sense of stillness.  Typing,
shifting in a chair, or a hand on a desk all register.  Respiration needs
motion below roughly +5 dB.  A recorded session where the subject believed they
were sitting still measured +12.8 to +14.4 dB throughout, and its spectrum had
no dominant peak anywhere in the band -- the detector was right to refuse.

WHAT A GOOD LOCK LOOKS LIKE.  motion under +5 dB, vitals above +20 dB, a rate
that drifts by tenths rather than jumping, and confidence holding above 0.4.

ONE REAL BUG WAS FOUND THIS WAY.  Chest motion does not modulate the channel
sinusoidally -- at close range the phase swing leaves the small-angle regime and
the response becomes rich in harmonics.  On a real subject the SEVENTH harmonic
measured 2.4 dB ABOVE the fundamental, and the old plain-argmax peak picker duly
reported a rate seven times too fast.  The fundamental is now chosen by
harmonic summation (score each candidate by the energy at f0, 2*f0, 3*f0 ...),
which is the standard pitch-detection technique: only the true fundamental has
all of its multiples lit up, while a harmonic's own multiples land on empty
spectrum.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6d. AIR QUALITY  --  what the number is, and what it is not
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The dashboard shows an AQI: a 0-500 index with the familiar categories, colours
and boundaries, computed with the same piecewise-linear breakpoint
interpolation a real AQI uses.

WHAT IS NOT STANDARD IS THE POLLUTANT, and this matters.

A regulatory AQI -- US EPA, or India's CPCB -- is the worst of several
sub-indices, and in practice PM2.5 dominates it. The MQ135 CANNOT MEASURE
PARTICULATES AT ALL. It is a single tin-dioxide resistance that responds to a
mix of reducing gases. So:

    A SMOKY ROOM CAN STILL READ "GOOD" HERE.
    A REAL MONITOR WOULD SCORE IT 150-200.

If you want a number that means what an air quality app means, you need a
particulate sensor -- a PMS5003 or SDS011. Those measure PM2.5 optically and
would slot into this system the same way these three did.

WHAT THE INDEX IS ACTUALLY BUILT FROM.  The MQ135's resistance is converted to
a CO2-equivalent ppm, then mapped onto the AQI scale at these breakpoints,
which are the established indoor ventilation thresholds:

      ppm CO2e        AQI      category
      ----------      -------  ------------------------------
        420 -  600      0 - 50  Good              (420 = outdoor air)
        600 - 1000     51 -100  Moderate
       1000 - 1500    101 -150  Unhealthy for sensitive groups
       1500 - 2000    151 -200  Unhealthy
       2000 - 5000    201 -300  Very unhealthy
       5000 -10000    301 -500  Hazardous

The raw ppm is always shown under the index for exactly this reason.

WHY IT SOMETIMES SHOWS A GREY "0" AND "BASELINE DRIFTED".

The AQI scale has no values below 0, and the lowest band starts at 420 ppm, so
anything at or below 420 ppm pins the index to exactly 0.  The scale floor
and the calibration anchor are deliberately the same number: at 400 a perfectly
calibrated sensor in genuinely outdoor air read AQI 5 rather than 0, so the
best reading the instrument can physically produce was not the bottom of its
own scale.

But a reading below 420 ppm indoors is not clean air -- it is IMPOSSIBLE. The
outdoor supply is the floor and people only add to it, so indoor CO2 is always
at or above outdoor. A reading underneath that says nothing about the air and
everything about the baseline: Rs has drifted above the R0 it was calibrated
against.

This happens constantly with a new sensor. Measured on this build one hour
after calibration: Rs climbed from 44,786 to 50,286 ohms -- up 12% -- and the
reported figure fell from 420 to 305 ppm. Nothing about the room had changed.

So below 378 ppm (10% under outdoor background, leaving room for noise) the
panel stops showing a reassuring green "Good" and instead greys the number out,
says "baseline drifted -- recalibrate", and switches the sparkline to plot raw
ppm rather than the pinned-at-zero index, because the drift is the actual
story. Showing the sensor's least trustworthy moment as its most reassuring one
would be the single most misleading thing this panel could do.

The fix is a recalibration once the element has settled -- see below.

CALIBRATION, AND WHY R0 IS NOT A CONSTANT.

Rs falls as gas rises, so the reading is a ratio Rs/R0 against a clean-air
reference. R0 is measured automatically after 3 minutes of warm-up, by assuming
the air at that moment is ordinary outdoor background (420 ppm).

A baseline captured once and then frozen DOES NOT WORK, and this build proved
it the hard way. A new MQ135's element resistance climbs for its first day or
two of continuous power. Measured here:

    at calibration      Rs 44,786 ohm   ->   420 ppm   AQI   5
    one hour later      Rs 50,286 ohm   ->   305 ppm   AQI   0
    two hours later     Rs 57,050 ohm   ->   215 ppm   AQI   0

Nothing about the room changed. The index sat flat at zero and the whole card
was dead, because the reading had fallen below the bottom of the scale and
stayed there.

AUTOMATIC BASELINE CORRECTION fixes this, and rests on one physical fact:
indoor air can never be cleaner than the outdoor air feeding it. Outdoor is the
floor; occupants only add to it. So if the CLEANEST air seen across a whole
10-minute window still reads below outdoor background, that is not clean air --
it is proof the baseline has drifted, and R0 is raised until that cleanest
sample reads as outdoor again.

This is the same principle commercial NDIR CO2 sensors use. Two deliberate
design choices in it:

  * The correction is ONE-WAY, upward only. Raising R0 can only make the room
    look dirtier, so the mechanism can never manufacture a reassuring reading.
    Lowering it would require knowing the air was genuinely clean, which cannot
    be known from the sensor alone -- in a continuously occupied room a
    two-directional tracker walks the baseline down until everything reads
    "fresh". Downward correction stays a deliberate act: "mq135 cal".

  * It tracks a SMOOTHED resistance, not raw samples. Since the ratchet is
    permanent, one noisy high sample would raise the baseline for good with
    nothing able to bring it back.

Set MQ135_ABC_ENABLE to 0 in config.h to switch it off, or change
MQ135_ABC_WINDOW_S to adjust how quickly it responds.

STILL DO A MANUAL CALIBRATION. Automatic correction keeps the reading on-scale
and honest; it does not make it accurate, because it can only ever assume the
cleanest air it has seen was outdoor air. For a real zero, put the node beside
an open window and run:

    cd pi && ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py mq135 cal

That treats the air right then as the 420 ppm reference and stores R0 in the
node's flash, so it survives reboots. Do it again after the sensor has had its
24-48 hours of initial burn-in.

The "env" command reports how many automatic corrections have been applied:

    mq135=952mV rs=49393 r0=71143 ratio=0.69 ~320ppm abc=0 5v
                                                        ^^^^^

TEMPERATURE AND HUMIDITY COMPENSATION.  The element's conductivity depends
strongly on both, which is normally a reason to distrust these sensors, because
the correction needs measurements the sensor cannot make. Here the DHT22 and
BMP280 are on the same board, so the correction is actually applied. In a hot,
humid room it is worth about 16% on Rs -- far from negligible. Set
MQ135_COMPENSATE to 0 in config.h to use the raw resistance instead.

A BUG WORTH KNOWING ABOUT, because the same trap is waiting in most MQ135 code
found online. There are TWO different definitions of R0 in circulation:

    * The datasheet's R0 is measured at 100 ppm ammonia. Against it, clean air
      sits at Rs/R0 ~= 3.6.
    * The ppm curve constants in general use (A = 116.6, B = 2.769) are fitted
      against an R0 measured at ATMOSPHERIC CO2.

Calibrating against one and evaluating with the other is self-consistent,
produces no error, no warning and no obviously broken value -- and is wrong by
two orders of magnitude. This build did exactly that at first and reported
4 ppm in ordinary room air. R0 is now derived by inverting the ppm curve at
420 ppm, which ties both ends to the same convention by construction.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.  READING THE DASHBOARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  BIG WORD          CLEAR / PRESENT / MOVEMENT / ACTIVE / VIGOROUS.
                    PRESENT means someone is there but not moving -- detected
                    by their breathing.

  MOTION  (dB)      gross movement. 0 dB is an empty room. Above ~6 dB is real
                    movement; walking typically reads 20-30 dB.

  VITALS  (dB)      breathing-band energy. This is what sees a still person.

  CHANNEL RESPONSE  the waterfall. Sub-carrier frequency up the side, time
                    running left to right, colour is signal strength.
                    Horizontal bands are the room's standing multipath
                    pattern; vertical streaks are something moving.

  ACTIVITY          both dB readings over the last minute. Dotted lines mark
                    the thresholds the system is actually deciding on.

  RESPIRATION       breaths per minute, the filtered waveform, and the spectrum
                    with the chosen peak highlighted. Blank with a flat line
                    is normal -- it needs a still subject and about 30 seconds.

  ENVIRONMENT       temperature, humidity, pressure and air quality, each with
                    a half-hour sparkline. The panel only appears once an
                    environment frame has actually arrived, so a build with no
                    sensors wired shows a dashboard that looks finished rather
                    than one full of dashes. A tick or cross per sensor sits in
                    the panel header.

                    TEMPERATURE shows the BMP280's reading, with both sensors'
                    figures underneath when both are present. They will
                    disagree by around a degree and that is expected: the
                    DHT22's humidity element sits in the same package and
                    self-heats, so it reads high. The BMP280 is the better
                    thermometer, which is why it wins.

                    AIR QUALITY is an AQI on the familiar 0-500 scale with the
                    standard categories and colours, computed by the same
                    piecewise-linear breakpoint interpolation a real AQI uses.
                    READ SECTION 6d BEFORE TRUSTING IT -- the pollutant behind
                    it is not the one a regulatory AQI is based on.

  PACKETS           every frame counter the system keeps, in four groups.

                    Four headline figures: DELIVERED (frames that arrived),
                    DELIVERY (the percentage that survived the link), LOST IN
                    TRANSIT and CORRUPTED.

                    Delivery is measured from the sequence number the node
                    stamps on every CSI frame, so a gap is a frame that
                    provably left the node and never arrived. That makes it real
                    loss rather than a slow reader -- unlike a rate estimate,
                    which cannot tell the two apart.

                    The colour is graded per transport, because "good" is not
                    the same number on each. A cable is lossless, so anything
                    under 99% on one means a bad lead. The nRF24 sits at ~88% by
                    design: a frame is five packets and survives only if all
                    five do, against a 3-deep hardware FIFO. 88% of 100 Hz is
                    still four times what respiration needs, so the radio's
                    green band starts at 80% rather than pretending a documented
                    normal is a fault.

                    BY FRAME TYPE separates csi / status / environment / log. A
                    single total cannot tell a link carrying CSI at 100 Hz from
                    one carrying only status frames once a second; this is the
                    group that makes a missing stream visible. It is also what
                    diagnosed the environment panel going blank against a node
                    running older firmware -- csi and status climbing with
                    environment stuck at zero.

                    INTEGRITY & FRAMING is CRC, COBS, short and unknown-type
                    rejects plus resyncs. All should stay at 0; the CRC catches
                    every corrupted frame, so these degrade the rate rather
                    than the correctness.

                    RADIO FRAGMENTS & ENCRYPTION appears only on a radio link
                    and is where frame loss is explained: fragment loss is
                    normally its cause. Duplicate fragments are auto-ack
                    retransmissions and are harmless. Note that fragment loss
                    and LOST IN TRANSIT are the same lost frames counted at two
                    layers -- do not add them together.

                    NODE, PIPELINE & STIMULUS is the two endpoints. The node's
                    own counters are the only view of what was *sent* rather
                    than what survived, which is what localises a fault to the
                    air rather than to either end. These count from when the
                    node last booted, not when the server started.

  DIAGNOSTICS       sample rate should sit near 100 Hz. Link errors, dropped
                    and rejected should all stay at 0.

CALIBRATION takes about 6 seconds for motion and 30 seconds for breathing.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7b. HISTORY AND THE SLEEP REPORT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    http://<pi-address>:8080/history        (or the HISTORY link in the header)

Everything on the live dashboard is live-only: the motion trace is 60 seconds,
the environment history half an hour, and a restart throws both away. That is
right for an instrument, but it means the thing this hardware is uniquely good
at -- watching a motionless person breathe, through a wall, with nothing worn --
could never be looked at afterwards. The archive is where that accumulates.

WHAT IS STORED

One row per 10 s holding aggregates over that bucket, not an instantaneous
sample. CSI arrives at 100 Hz; storing that would be 8.6 million rows a day to
describe something that moves over minutes. 10 s costs ~8,600 rows a day, which
SQLite does not notice, and 60 days of it is around 30 MB.

Each row keeps BOTH the mean and the peak of motion_db, and that pairing is the
point. A 10 s mean hides a one-second movement spike completely -- exactly the
event that separates "asleep" from "turned over" -- while the peak alone would
call a single twitch a restless night. On the history page the mean is the line
and the peak is the lighter band above it.

Environment is sampled once a minute, because the sensor itself only reports
every 3 s and a room's temperature and CO2 do not move faster than that.

Settings live in pi/config.json: archive_enabled, archive_db,
archive_interval_s, archive_env_interval_s, archive_retain_days (0 keeps
everything). A write failure never stops the node sensing -- a full disk
degrades this feature to nothing and leaves detection untouched.

THE SLEEP REPORT

A night is named by the EVENING it began, so the night of the 13th spans 21:00
on the 13th to 11:00 on the 14th. Asked in the morning, the page defaults to
the night that just ended. The window is sleep_window_start_h and
sleep_window_end_h.

It reports time in bed, still time, restless time, disturbances with their
times and durations, and breathing rate over the night with min/max and how
much of the night held a lock.

  * TIME IN BED is the longest occupied stretch, merging absences under 20
    minutes -- so a trip to the bathroom does not end the night, while an
    evening on the sofa does not merge into it. An 8 h night broken by a 70 min
    absence reports the longer 230 min segment, not 480.

  * STILL means gross motion stayed under +5 dB. Not an arbitrary threshold:
    section 6c establishes that respiration only resolves below roughly +5 dB,
    so it is the same line the breathing detector already works to.

  * A DISTURBANCE has to last 2 minutes. Below that it is a turn-over, and
    counting those inflates the number into meaninglessness.

  * Only VALID breathing estimates are averaged. An invalid one carries a bpm
    of 0.0, and folding those in would drag every night's mean toward zero in
    proportion to how often the lock dropped.

WHAT IT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT REPORT: SLEEP STAGES.

No REM, no deep, no light. Those are distinguished by brain and eye activity,
which is not present in this signal at any level -- a radio path through a wall
measures movement and chest wall motion and nothing else. Every consumer
device that prints a stage breakdown from movement alone is inferring it from a
model, not measuring it. This reports stillness, which is what it measures, and
leaves whether that stillness was sleep to the reader.

It is also not a medical device. It is a bedroom instrument built from a $5
microcontroller.

A night with no data says so rather than reporting zero sleep, and a period
under 45 minutes is refused rather than presented as a night.

REST endpoints, if you want the numbers elsewhere:

    /api/history?hours=24&points=900     archived series
    /api/history/stats                   rows held, span, write errors
    /api/sleep?night=YYYY-MM-DD          one night's report (default last night)
    /api/sleep/nights                    which nights hold data


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.  GETTING GOOD RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Put the ESP32 and the WiFi router on OPPOSITE SIDES of the space you want
    to watch. You are sensing what happens between them, not around the ESP32.
    This matters more than anything else.

  * 2 to 8 metres apart works well.

  * A stronger signal is not automatically better. Very close together, the
    direct path dominates and small changes get lost in it. RSSI around -40 to
    -60 dBm is a good working range.

  * Breathing detection needs the subject reasonably still and reasonably
    close, and about 30 seconds of settling.

  * Ceiling fans, moving curtains and pets all register as motion. They are not
    false positives -- something really is moving.

  * A second ESP32 node would be the single biggest upgrade: it enables the
    CSI-ratio method, which cancels the oscillator errors that currently make
    phase unusable, and materially improves breathing sensitivity.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.  TROUBLESHOOTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

NO SERIAL DEVICE
    ls /dev/ttyUSB*
    Nothing? Try a different USB cable -- charge-only cables are the usual
    cause. Then check: lsusb | grep -i "silicon labs"

SAMPLE RATE IS ~1 Hz INSTEAD OF ~100 Hz
    The node is not associated, so the Pi's UDP stimulus cannot reach it.
        cd pi && ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py info
    Look for assoc=yes and an IP. If not:
      - is the SSID 2.4 GHz? The ESP32-S3 cannot see 5 GHz networks
      - re-provision: tools/node.py wifi "SSID" "password"

SAMPLE RATE IS 0
    Check csi=ok in tools/node.py info. If it says FAILED the CSI API was
    rejected, which usually means a firmware/IDF mismatch -- reflash.

EVERYTHING READS "CLEAR" WITH SOMEONE IN THE ROOM
    Are the node and the router on opposite sides of that person? If the person
    is not between them there is little to disturb.
    Check RSSI is in the -40 to -60 range.

EVERYTHING READS "ACTIVE" IN AN EMPTY ROOM
    Something is moving: a fan, an open window, a pet. Also check that the
    node is physically stable -- a board swinging on its cable is motion.

BREATHING NEVER RESOLVES
    It needs: someone still, 30+ seconds, and a sample rate above ~20 Hz.
    "subject moving" in the panel means the motion detector is vetoing it,
    which is correct behaviour rather than a fault.

LINK ERRORS CLIMBING
    Serial corruption. Try a shorter or better USB cable. The CRC catches every
    corrupted frame, so this degrades the rate rather than the correctness.

SERVICE WILL NOT START
    journalctl -u wifisense -n 40

ENVIRONMENT PANEL NEVER APPEARS
    No environment frame has arrived at all. Ask the node directly:
        cd pi && ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py env
    It reports each sensor separately, so this immediately tells you whether
    the problem is one sensor or the whole path.

    If the node answers with good readings but the panel still never appears,
    check the PACKETS panel: csi and status climbing while ENVIRONMENT sits at
    zero means the frames are arriving and being rejected rather than never
    being sent.

    That is a wire-format version mismatch. The environment frame grew from 26
    to 31 bytes when R0 and the automatic-baseline count were added, and a Pi
    expecting only the longer one discards every frame from a node still
    running older firmware -- silently, as "short frames", because a truncated
    payload and an older payload look identical. The Pi now decodes both
    lengths, reporting r0 and abc as 0 on the legacy format; nothing else is
    affected, since those two fields are metadata and the AQI does not rest on
    them. Reflashing the node restores them.

BMP280 = ABSENT
    The chip id read did not come back. In order of likelihood:
      - SDO and SDA swapped. In SPI mode SDA is the INPUT and SDO the OUTPUT,
        which is the opposite of what the I2C-style labels suggest.
      - CSB not on GPIO15, or left floating.
      - The module is an I2C-only breakout. Some BMP280 boards tie CSB high on
        the PCB and never enter SPI mode. Check for a solder jumper.

DHT22 = NO-REPLY
    An occasional miss is normal and is retried silently; a rising count is
    not. Check:
      - a bare 4-pin sensor needs a 10 kOhm pull-up from DATA to 3V3. The
        3-pin breakout boards already have one.
      - DATA on GPIO16, VCC on 3V3 not 5V.
      - some counterfeit AM2302s need >2 s between reads; raise
        ENV_INTERVAL_MS in config.h to 5000 and see if the count stops rising.

MQ135 = OUT-OF-RANGE
    The reported millivolts tell you which end it failed at.
      near 0 mV      AO not connected, or the divider's lower resistor is
                     shorted to ground
      near supply    the sensing element is saturated, or AO is wired straight
                     to 5 V with no divider -- CHECK THIS FIRST, it can damage
                     the ADC input
    If the module is powered from 3V3 rather than 5 V, tell the node:
        python tools/node.py mq135 supply 3v3

AIR QUALITY STUCK AT "WARMING UP"
    Auto-calibration needs 3 minutes of continuous power AND a valid reading.
    If the millivolt figure is moving but calibration never happens, the
    reading is failing its range check -- see the previous entry.

AIR QUALITY SHOWS A GREY 0 AND "BASELINE DRIFTED"
    The reading has fallen below outdoor background, which is impossible for
    real air -- the sensor's baseline has drifted above where it calibrated.
    Normal for the first 24-48 hours of a new sensor. Recalibrate by an open
    window once it has settled:
        cd pi && ../.venv/bin/python tools/node.py mq135 cal

AIR QUALITY LOOKS TOO LOW / ALWAYS "GOOD"
    Expected if it calibrated indoors: the baseline was anchored to whatever
    the room was at that moment. Recalibrate by an open window:
        python tools/node.py mq135 cal
    Also read section 6d -- this index cannot see PM2.5 at all, so it will
    genuinely read "Good" in a smoky room.
